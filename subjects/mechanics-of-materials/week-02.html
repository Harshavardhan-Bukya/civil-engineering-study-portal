03

Ductility

Ductility is expressed in the notes using percentage elongation and percentage reduction in area.

1. Percentage Elongation

% Elongation = [(Lf − L0) / L0] × 100

Where:

  • L₀ = original gauge length
  • Lf = length of specimen after breaking

2. Percentage Reduction in Area

% Reduction in Area = [(A0 − Af) / A0] × 100

Where:

  • A₀ = original cross-sectional area
  • Af = cross-sectional area after breaking
Both percentage elongation and percentage reduction in area are used as measures of ductility.

Stress-Strain Behaviour of Different Materials

1. Linear Elastic Material

Stress is proportional to strain in the linear elastic region.

σ ∝ ε
σ = Eε

2. Non-Linear Elastic Material

The notes show a curved stress-strain relationship rather than a straight-line relationship.

3. Elastic-Plastic Material

The material first behaves elastically and then enters the plastic region.

Elastic-Plastic Behaviour

4. Elastic-Plastic Material with Strain Hardening

After the elastic region, the material enters plastic deformation and then shows strain hardening.

Strain hardening increases the stress required for further plastic deformation.

5. Perfectly Plastic / Ideal Plastic Material

The notes show a horizontal plastic region after yielding.

Perfectly Plastic Behaviour

The notes state that a sudden load is applied and the constant load can be supported after yielding.

Ideal Material

The notes describe an ideal material as a rigid material that does not undergo deformation under the applied stress.

E = Stress / Strain
For an ideal rigid material, strain is zero and the notes indicate an infinitely large Young's modulus.

Ideal Fluid

The notes state:

  • Ideal fluid has no viscosity.
  • Shear resistance is zero.
  • Young's modulus for an ideal fluid is zero.
06

Factor of Safety

The notes define factor of safety as the ratio between strength and working stress.

F.S. = Strength / Working Stress

Another form shown in the notes is:

F.S. = Maximum permissible stress / Working stress

For Steel

F.S. = Yield Stress / Working Stress

For Brittle Materials

F.S. = Ultimate Stress / Working Stress
The notes state that the factor of safety should be greater than 1.

Example

F.S. = 130 / 80 = 1.625

The handwritten example in the notes is approximately shown as 1.5.

08

Young's Modulus (E)

Young's modulus is the ratio of normal stress to longitudinal strain.

E = Normal Stress / Longitudinal Strain
E = σ / ε

Use in Deformation

δ = PL / AE

The notes also show:

δ = WL³ / 48EI

Stiffness

K = P / δ
K = AE / L
The notes indicate that stiffness is directly proportional to E and therefore increases when E increases.

Values of Young's Modulus Mentioned in the Notes

Material Young's Modulus
Aluminium 70 GPa
Cast Iron 100 GPa
Mild Steel 200 GPa
Diamond 1200 GPa
The notes also indicate that the units may be expressed as N/mm² or Pa depending on the unit system.
09

Modulus of Rigidity / Shear Modulus (G)

Modulus of rigidity is the ratio of shear stress to shear strain.

G = Shear Stress / Shear Strain
G = τ / γ

The notes also relate shear strain to twisting or angular deformation.

Unit commonly used in the notes: N/mm².
10

Bulk Modulus (K)

Bulk modulus is the ratio of direct stress to volumetric strain.

K = Direct Stress / Volumetric Strain
K = σ / εv
Unit = N/mm²

The notes state that in all directions an equal amount of stress is generated for bulk deformation.

11

Poisson's Ratio (μ)

Poisson's ratio is the factor which represents the effect of longitudinal force in the lateral direction.

μ = Lateral Strain / Longitudinal Strain
μ = εy / εx = εz / εx
Poisson's ratio has no units.

Range Mentioned in the Notes

  • For any material: approximately −1 to 0.5
  • For engineering materials: approximately 0 to 0.5
  • For generic materials: approximately −1 to 0
  • For steel: approximately 0.3
  • For incompressible materials: μ = 0.5

Notes on Material Behaviour

  • For clay and rubber, the notes mention μ ≈ 0.5.
  • The notes indicate that for steel, μ is approximately 0.3.

13

Numerical Problems

Numerical 1 — Find Poisson's Ratio

Given:

Young's modulus: E = 200 GPa

Modulus of rigidity: G = 80 GPa

Find Poisson's ratio μ.

Step 1: Use:
E = 2G(1 + μ)
Step 2: Substitute:
200 = 2(80)(1 + μ)
Step 3:
200 = 160(1 + μ)
200 = 160 + 160μ
160μ = 40
μ = 40/160
μ = 0.25

Numerical 2 — Find Modulus of Rigidity

Given:

Young's modulus: E = 120 GPa

Poisson's ratio: μ = 0.25

Find modulus of rigidity G.

Step 1:
E = 2G(1 + μ)
Step 2:
120 = 2G(1 + 0.25)
120 = 2.5G
G = 48 GPa
The handwritten final figure on the supplied page is difficult to read; using the displayed formula and given values gives G = 48 GPa.

Formula Revision

E = 2G(1 + μ)
E = 3K(1 − 2μ)
E = 9KG / (3K + G)
μ = Lateral Strain / Longitudinal Strain
