TOPIC 01

Introduction to Fluids & Fluid Mechanics

Fluid Mechanics is the branch of engineering science which deals with the behaviour of fluids, both liquids and gases, at rest and in motion.

What is a Fluid?

A fluid is a substance which undergoes continuous deformation when subjected to shear stress, however small the applied shear stress may be.

Liquids and gases are therefore classified as fluids.

Main Divisions of Fluid Mechanics

Fluid Statics

Fluid statics deals with fluids which are at rest.

Fluid Kinematics

Fluid kinematics deals with fluids in motion without considering the forces responsible for producing the motion.

Fluid Dynamics

Fluid dynamics deals with fluids in motion while considering the forces and pressure effects responsible for the motion.

Fluid

A substance capable of continuous deformation under shear stress.

Liquids and Gases

A liquid possesses a definite volume but does not possess a definite shape. It assumes the shape of the container in which it is placed.

A gas possesses neither a definite shape nor a definite volume and expands to occupy the available space.

Ideal and Real Fluids

An ideal fluid is an imaginary fluid having zero viscosity and is generally considered incompressible.

A real fluid possesses viscosity. All actual engineering fluids are real fluids.

⭐ Important for examination: Definition of fluid and differences between fluid statics, fluid kinematics and fluid dynamics.

TOPIC 02

Density, Specific Weight, Specific Volume & Specific Gravity

1. Mass Density

Mass density is defined as the mass of a fluid contained per unit volume.
ρ = m / V

Where:

ρ = mass density in kg/m³
m = mass in kg
V = volume in m³

For water:

ρw ≈ 1000 kg/m³

2. Specific Weight

Specific weight, also called weight density, is the weight of fluid per unit volume.

w = W / V

Since:

W = mg

Therefore:

w = ρg

SI unit:

N/m³

For water:

w ≈ 9810 N/m³

3. Specific Volume

Specific volume is the volume occupied by unit mass of fluid.

v = V/m = 1/ρ

SI unit:

m³/kg

4. Specific Gravity

Specific gravity is the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a standard fluid.

For liquids, the standard fluid is water.

S = ρfluid / ρwater

It can also be written as:

S = wfluid / wwater

Specific gravity has no unit.

Useful Relations

ρliquid = S × 1000 kg/m³
wliquid = S × 9810 N/m³

Important Conversion

1 litre = 10⁻³ m³

Worked Numerical

Problem: One litre of a liquid weighs 7 N. Find its specific weight, density and specific gravity.

Given:

W = 7 N

V = 1 litre = 0.001 m³

Specific Weight:

w = W/V
w = 7/0.001 = 7000 N/m³

Density:

ρ = w/g
ρ = 7000/9.81
ρ = 713.6 kg/m³

Specific Gravity:

S = 713.6/1000
S = 0.714

Final Answer:

Specific weight = 7000 N/m³
Density = 713.6 kg/m³
Specific gravity = 0.714

⭐ Specific gravity is dimensionless. ⭐ Specific volume is the reciprocal of density. ⭐ w = ρg is a fundamental relation.

Marks-wise Questions

3 Marks

Density: Mass per unit volume. ρ = m/V. SI unit: kg/m³.

Specific volume: Volume per unit mass. v = V/m = 1/ρ. SI unit: m³/kg.

Specific weight: Weight per unit volume. w = W/V = ρg. SI unit: N/m³.

Since ρw = 1000 kg/m³:

ρ = 1000S

Similarly:

S = w/ww

Therefore:

Use:

TOPIC 03

Viscosity & Newton's Law of Viscosity

Viscosity is the property of a fluid by virtue of which it offers resistance to the movement of one layer of fluid over an adjacent layer.

Physical Meaning

When fluid layers move at different velocities, internal friction develops between adjacent layers. This resistance to relative motion is called viscosity.

Newton's Law of Viscosity

Consider two adjacent fluid layers separated by a small distance dy.

Let the difference in velocity between the layers be du.

The velocity gradient is:

du/dy

According to Newton's law of viscosity:

τ ∝ du/dy

Therefore:

τ = μ(du/dy)

Meaning of Symbols

τ = shear stress, N/m²

μ = dynamic viscosity

du/dy = velocity gradient, s⁻¹

Dynamic Viscosity

Rearranging Newton's law:

μ = τ / (du/dy)

Dynamic viscosity may therefore be interpreted as the shear stress required to produce unit rate of shear strain.

SI Unit

N·s/m²

This is equivalent to:

Pa·s = kg/(m·s)

CGS Unit

Poise (P)

Conversions

1 P = 0.1 Pa·s
1 Pa·s = 10 P
1 cP = 0.01 P
1 cP = 0.001 Pa·s
⭐ Newton's law of viscosity:
τ = μ(du/dy)
This is one of the most important formulas in Properties of Fluids.

Marks-wise Questions

2 Marks

Define viscosity.

Viscosity is the property of a fluid by virtue of which it offers resistance to the relative motion of adjacent fluid layers.

6 Marks

State and explain Newton's law of viscosity. Also write the units of dynamic viscosity.

Newton's law states that shear stress is directly proportional to the velocity gradient.

τ ∝ du/dy
τ = μ(du/dy)

Where τ is shear stress, μ is dynamic viscosity and du/dy is velocity gradient.

SI unit of μ: N·s/m² or Pa·s.

CGS unit: Poise.

TOPIC 04

Kinematic Viscosity & Viscosity Numericals

Kinematic Viscosity

Kinematic viscosity is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to mass density.

ν = μ/ρ

Where:

ν = kinematic viscosity
μ = dynamic viscosity
ρ = mass density

SI Unit

m²/s

CGS Unit

Stoke (St)

Conversions

1 St = 1 cm²/s
1 St = 10⁻⁴ m²/s
1 cSt = 10⁻⁶ m²/s

Numerical Type 1 — Moving Plate

If a plate moves with velocity u and the fluid layer has thickness y, for a linear velocity distribution:

du/dy = u/y

Using Newton's law:

τ = μu/y

Therefore:

μ = τy/u

Numerical Procedure

Step 1 — Given

Write velocity, distance between plates, shear stress and any density data.

Step 2 — Unit Conversion

Convert millimetres to metres and viscosity units to SI units.

Step 3 — Formula

τ = μ(du/dy)

Step 4 — Substitution

Substitute the known values.

Step 5 — Final Answer

Write μ in Pa·s or ν in m²/s as required.

Velocity Distribution Problems

If velocity is given as:

u = f(y)

Differentiate the equation:

du/dy = d[f(y)]/dy

Then apply:

τ = μ(du/dy)
⭐ For viscosity numericals always use: Given → Conversion → Formula → Substitution → Calculation → Final Answer.

Marks-wise Questions

3 Marks

Define kinematic viscosity. Write its formula and units.

Kinematic viscosity is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to mass density.

ν = μ/ρ

SI unit = m²/s.
CGS unit = Stoke.

5 Marks

Explain how shear stress is determined when the velocity distribution u = f(y) is given.

First differentiate the velocity equation with respect to y to obtain the velocity gradient.

du/dy

Evaluate the gradient at the required location and substitute it in Newton's law:

τ = μ(du/dy)

The resulting value gives the shear stress.

TOPIC 05

Classification and Types of Fluids

Ideal Fluid

An ideal fluid is an imaginary fluid having zero viscosity.

Real Fluid

A real fluid possesses viscosity. All actual fluids are real fluids.

Newtonian Fluid

A Newtonian fluid obeys Newton's law of viscosity.

τ = μ(du/dy)

For a Newtonian fluid, μ remains constant at a specified temperature and pressure.

Non-Newtonian Fluid

A non-Newtonian fluid does not obey the simple linear relationship between shear stress and velocity gradient.

Ideal Plastic Fluid

An ideal plastic fluid requires a certain minimum shear stress called yield stress before continuous deformation begins.

Bingham Plastic

A Bingham plastic behaves approximately as a rigid body until the yield stress is exceeded. After yielding, shear stress varies approximately linearly with the rate of shear strain.

Pseudoplastic Fluid

A pseudoplastic fluid is a shear-thinning fluid. Its apparent viscosity decreases as shear rate increases.

Dilatant Fluid

A dilatant fluid is a shear-thickening fluid. Its apparent viscosity increases as shear rate increases.

⭐ Important graph: Shear stress τ is plotted on the vertical axis and velocity gradient du/dy on the horizontal axis.

Marks-wise Questions

5 Marks

Define ideal, real, Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids.

An ideal fluid has zero viscosity. A real fluid possesses viscosity. A Newtonian fluid obeys τ = μdu/dy. A non-Newtonian fluid does not follow a linear proportionality between shear stress and velocity gradient.

8 Marks

Explain the rheological classification of fluids using shear stress versus velocity-gradient behaviour.

Plot shear stress τ on the vertical axis and du/dy on the horizontal axis.

An ideal fluid has zero shear stress. A Newtonian fluid gives a straight line through the origin.

Plastic-type fluids require a yield stress before continuous deformation.

Pseudoplastic fluids are shear-thinning, whereas dilatant fluids are shear-thickening.

TOPIC 06

Effect of Temperature on Viscosity

Liquids

The viscosity of liquids generally decreases when temperature increases.

This occurs because the cohesive forces between liquid molecules become less effective as temperature rises.

Temperature ↑ → μ of liquid ↓

Gases

The viscosity of gases generally increases when temperature increases.

Higher temperature increases molecular motion and momentum transfer between gas layers.

Temperature ↑ → μ of gas ↑

Effect of Pressure

For ordinary engineering pressure ranges, the effect of pressure on liquid viscosity is usually small.

At very high pressures, however, the effect may become significant.

⭐ Remember the opposite temperature behaviour: Liquids: temperature ↑ → viscosity ↓
Gases: temperature ↑ → viscosity ↑

Marks-wise Questions

4 Marks

Explain the effect of temperature on the viscosity of liquids and gases.

For liquids, viscosity decreases with increasing temperature because cohesive forces become weaker.

For gases, viscosity increases with increasing temperature because molecular momentum transfer increases.

2 Marks

State the effect of pressure on viscosity.

At ordinary engineering pressures the effect of pressure on liquid viscosity is generally small, but it can become significant at very high pressures.

TOPIC 07

Surface Tension & Capillarity

Surface Tension

Surface tension is the tensile force acting per unit length in the free surface of a liquid, normal to an imaginary line drawn in that surface.
σ = F/L

Where:

σ = surface tension
F = force
L = length

SI Unit

N/m

Cause of Surface Tension

A molecule inside a liquid is attracted by surrounding molecules in all directions.

A molecule near the free surface experiences an unbalanced inward molecular attraction.

As a result, the free surface behaves like a stretched elastic membrane.

Capillarity

Capillarity is the phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a small-diameter tube relative to the surrounding liquid level.

Capillary Rise Equation

h = 4σ cosθ / (ρgd)

Since w = ρg:

h = 4σ cosθ / (wd)

Meaning of Symbols

h = capillary rise or fall, m
σ = surface tension, N/m
θ = angle of contact
ρ = density, kg/m³
d = diameter of capillary tube, m

Water in Glass

For clean water and glass:

θ ≈ 0°

Therefore:

cosθ ≈ 1

Water rises in the capillary tube.

Mercury in Glass

Mercury generally forms an obtuse angle of contact with glass and therefore produces capillary depression.

Derivation of Capillary Rise

Upward vertical component of surface tension:

F = πdσ cosθ

Weight of liquid column:

W = ρg(πd²/4)h

At equilibrium:

πdσ cosθ = ρg(πd²/4)h

Therefore:

h = 4σ cosθ/(ρgd)
⭐ Capillary rise is inversely proportional to tube diameter. Smaller diameter → greater capillary rise.

Marks-wise Questions

3 Marks

Define surface tension and state its SI unit.

Surface tension is the tensile force per unit length acting at the free surface of a liquid.

σ = F/L

SI unit = N/m.

8 Marks

Derive the expression for capillary rise in a circular tube.

Upward surface-tension force:

πdσ cosθ

Weight of liquid column:

ρg(πd²/4)h

Equating:

πdσ cosθ = ρg(πd²/4)h

Therefore:

h = 4σ cosθ/(ρgd)

TOPIC 08

Pressure Inside Liquid Droplets, Soap Bubbles & Liquid Jets

Surface tension produces an excess pressure inside curved liquid surfaces.

1. Liquid Droplet

A liquid droplet has one liquid-gas interface.

Δp = 4σ/d

Since d = 2r:

Δp = 2σ/r

2. Soap Bubble

A soap bubble has two liquid surfaces: an inner surface and an outer surface.

Δp = 8σ/d

or:

Δp = 4σ/r

3. Liquid Jet

Δp = 2σ/d

or:

Δp = σ/r

Comparison

For the same diameter and surface tension:

Soap Bubble: 8σ/d

Liquid Droplet: 4σ/d

Liquid Jet: 2σ/d

Numerical Method

Given: σ and diameter d.

Step 1: Convert diameter into metres.

Step 2: Identify whether it is a droplet, soap bubble or liquid jet.

Step 3: Select the correct formula.

Step 4: Substitute the values.

Step 5: Write the final pressure in Pa.

⭐ For equal σ and d: Soap bubble pressure > liquid droplet pressure > liquid jet pressure.

Marks-wise Questions

6 Marks

Derive the expression for excess pressure inside a liquid droplet.

Consider one half of a spherical droplet.

Pressure force:

Δp × πd²/4

Surface-tension force:

σπd

At equilibrium:

Δp(πd²/4) = σπd

Therefore:

Δp = 4σ/d
8 Marks

Write and compare the expressions for excess pressure inside a liquid droplet, soap bubble and liquid jet.

Liquid droplet:

Δp = 4σ/d

Soap bubble:

Δp = 8σ/d

Liquid jet:

Δp = 2σ/d

For equal surface tension and diameter, the soap bubble has the highest excess pressure.

TOPIC 09

Compressibility & Bulk Modulus

Compressibility

Although liquids are frequently treated as incompressible in elementary fluid mechanics, their volume changes slightly when pressure changes.

Bulk Modulus

Bulk modulus is the ratio of increase in pressure to the corresponding volumetric strain.

K = -Δp / (ΔV/V)

Meaning of Symbols

K = bulk modulus, Pa

Δp = change in pressure, Pa

ΔV = change in volume

V = original volume

Why is the Negative Sign Used?

When pressure increases, the volume of a fluid decreases.

Therefore ΔV is negative for an increase in pressure. The negative sign makes the value of bulk modulus positive.

Compressibility

Compressibility is the reciprocal of bulk modulus.

β = 1/K

Interpretation

A large value of bulk modulus means that the fluid is difficult to compress.

Therefore:

Large K → Low Compressibility

Effect on Density

For a fixed mass:

ρ = m/V

If pressure increases and volume decreases, density increases.

⭐ Bulk modulus has the same SI unit as pressure: N/m² or Pa.

Marks-wise Questions

5 Marks

Define bulk modulus and compressibility.

Bulk modulus is the ratio of pressure change to the corresponding volumetric strain.

K = -Δp/(ΔV/V)

Compressibility is the reciprocal of bulk modulus.

β = 1/K
6 Marks

Explain the significance of the negative sign in the bulk modulus equation.

An increase in pressure causes a decrease in volume. Therefore the volume change ΔV is negative. The negative sign in the bulk-modulus expression ensures that K is expressed as a positive property.

TOPIC 10

Vapour Pressure & Chapter Formula Revision

Vapour Pressure

At a given temperature, some molecules near the surface of a liquid possess sufficient energy to escape from the liquid into the vapour phase.

In a closed space, a condition of equilibrium may be established between evaporation and condensation.

The pressure exerted by the vapour when the liquid and vapour are in equilibrium at a specified temperature is called the vapour pressure of the liquid.

Effect of Temperature

Vapour pressure depends strongly on temperature. It generally increases when temperature increases.

Engineering Significance

If the local absolute pressure in a flowing liquid falls sufficiently close to or below the vapour pressure of the liquid, vapour formation may occur.

This principle becomes important later when studying cavitation.

Chapter 1 Formula Revision

ρ = m/V
w = ρg
v = 1/ρ
S = ρ/ρw
τ = μ(du/dy)
ν = μ/ρ
Droplet: Δp = 4σ/d
Soap Bubble: Δp = 8σ/d
Liquid Jet: Δp = 2σ/d
h = 4σ cosθ/(ρgd)
K = -Δp/(ΔV/V)

Marks-wise Questions

3 Marks

Define vapour pressure.

Vapour pressure is the equilibrium pressure exerted by vapour above its liquid at a specified temperature.

5 Marks

Explain the engineering significance of vapour pressure.

Vapour pressure indicates the pressure level at which a liquid can begin forming vapour at a particular temperature.

If the local absolute pressure falls to the vapour-pressure level, vapour formation can occur. This concept is important in the study of cavitation.
