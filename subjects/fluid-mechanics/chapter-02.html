TOPIC 01

Fluid Pressure at a Point

Pressure at a point: pressure is the normal force acting per unit area at a point in a fluid.
p = dF / dA
  • p = pressure
  • dF = small normal force
  • dA = small area
For uniform pressure:   p = F / A
Units given in the reference
  • N/m² or N/m² in SI
  • 1 Pa = 1 N/m²
  • 1 kPa = 1000 N/m²
  • 1 bar = 100 kPa
Concept diagram — pressure force on a fluid element
pₓpₓpᵧpᵧ

For a small fluid element, pressure forces act normal to its faces.

⭐ Main point: pressure is a normal force per unit area.

TOPIC 02

Pascal's Law

Pascal's law: the pressure or intensity of pressure at a point in a static fluid is equal in all directions.
  • Consider a very small fluid element.
  • Pressure acts normal to the surfaces.
  • Resolving the pressure forces in different directions gives equal pressure components.
pₓ = pᵧ = p𝓏
Meaning: pressure at a point in a static fluid is the same in x, y and z directions.
Hydraulic press — application of Pascal's law

The reference uses Pascal's law to show that the pressure developed at the small piston is transmitted through the fluid and acts at the large piston.

TOPIC 03

Pressure Variation in a Fluid at Rest

Hydrostatic pressure: pressure in a fluid at rest varies with vertical position because of the weight of the fluid.
Forces on a small fluid element
  • Pressure force on the lower face.
  • Pressure force on the upper face.
  • Weight of the fluid element.
  • The element is in equilibrium.
dp/dz = −ρg

Here z is measured vertically upward in the reference convention.

dp = ρg dz   (when depth is measured downward)
p = p₀ + ρgz

where ρ is density, g is acceleration due to gravity and z is the depth below the reference/free surface according to the depth convention.

Pressure head = p / (ρg)

Pressure increases as depth increases.

Problems 2.3–2.8: pressure at a given depth for water, oil, mercury and fluids with specified specific gravity; conversion of pressure into equivalent head of water/oil.

Important Questions

3 Marks

State the hydrostatic law.

For a fluid at rest, dp/dz = −ρg when z is measured upward.

5 Marks

Derive the pressure variation in a fluid at rest.

Take a small fluid element, write the pressure forces on its faces and its weight, apply equilibrium, obtain dp/dz = −ρg, and integrate to obtain the pressure relation.

3 Marks

Define pressure head.

Pressure head is p/(ρg).

5 Marks

Explain how pressure changes with depth.

For a fluid at rest, pressure increases as depth increases because the fluid above contributes its weight.

Absolute, Gauge, Atmospheric and Vacuum Pressures

Absolute pressure

Pressure measured with reference to absolute zero pressure.

Gauge pressure

Pressure measured with atmospheric pressure as the reference.

Atmospheric pressure

Pressure exerted by the atmosphere.

Vacuum pressure

Pressure below atmospheric pressure.

6.1 Piezometer

  • A simple pressure-measuring arrangement.
  • A small tube is connected to the point where pressure is to be measured.
  • The liquid rises in the tube.

6.2 U-tube Manometer

The reference gives a U-tube with a heavy liquid. It can be used for gauge pressure and vacuum pressure.

Gauge pressure arrangement

Pressure at A is related to the difference in liquid-column levels.

Vacuum pressure arrangement

The column arrangement is used when pressure at A is below atmospheric pressure.

U-tube method: write pressure at one level, move through the connected liquids using ρgh changes, and equate pressures at the same horizontal level.

6.3 Single Column Manometer

  • It is a modified form of a U-tube manometer.
  • One limb is a large reservoir and the other is a small tube.
  • Types in the reference: vertical single column and inclined single column manometer.
Pressure relation uses the reservoir area, tube area, liquid densities and the change in liquid levels.
Single-column manometer concept

The large reservoir makes the level change in it small compared with the tube.

Differential Manometers

Differential manometers are used for measuring the difference of pressures between two points in a pipe or between two different pipes.

7.1 U-tube differential manometer

  • Contains a manometric liquid in a U-tube.
  • The two pressure points may be at different levels.
  • The two points may also be at the same level.
  • Pressure equations are obtained by moving through the liquids and equating pressures at the same datum.
U-tube differential manometer

The pressure difference between A and B is obtained from the liquid levels and densities.

7.2 Inverted U-tube differential manometer

  • An inverted U-tube arrangement is used for pressure difference measurement.
  • The reference derives the pressure relation by considering the liquids in the two limbs.
  • The arrangement can be used for two pressure points containing liquids of different densities.

TOPIC 08

Pressure at a Point in a Compressible Fluid

For compressible fluids, density changes with pressure and temperature. The reference develops pressure variation with height using the equation of state and specified processes.

8.1 Basic equation of state

p = ρRT

The reference uses the gas equation in the derivation of pressure variation.

8.2 Isothermal process

  • Temperature T is constant.
  • The reference combines the gas equation with the hydrostatic relation.
  • The pressure variation with height is obtained by integration.
p / p₀ = e−gz/(RT)   (isothermal form used in the reference)

8.3 Adiabatic process

p / ρk = C
  • Temperature is not constant.
  • The reference combines the adiabatic relation with the hydrostatic equation.
  • The resulting pressure relation is expressed using k, R, g, z, p₀ and ρ₀.
p = p₀ [1 − ((k−1)/k)(ρ₀/p₀)gz]k/(k−1)

8.4 Temperature at any point in a compressible fluid

T = T₀ [1 − ((k−1)/k)(gz/(RT₀))]

The reference derives temperature at height z by combining pressure-density and gas relations.

8.5 Temperature lapse rate

L = −dT/dz = g(k−1)/(kR)
  • L = temperature lapse-rate parameter.
  • For k = 1, the temperature is constant.
  • For k > 1, temperature decreases with increase of height.
