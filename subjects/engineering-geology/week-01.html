TOPIC 01

Branches of Geology & Importance in Civil Engineering

The branches of geology introduced in the Week 01 class notes and their engineering significance.

1. Physical Geology

Definition: Physical Geology is described in the class notes as Dynamic Geology or Geomorphology and deals with the different physical features of the Earth.

It deals with physical features such as:

  • Mountains
  • Rivers
  • Lakes
  • Glaciers
  • Volcanoes

It also deals with changes occurring on the Earth's surface, including the formation or disappearance of rivers, springs and lakes.

Natural phenomena considered include:

  • Slides and landslides
  • Earthquakes
  • Weathering

The geological work of wind, glaciers, oceans and groundwater continuously modifies the Earth's surface.

⭐ Exam Point: Physical Geology deals with physical features of the Earth and processes that modify its surface.

2. Mineralogy

Mineralogy deals with the study of minerals.

It includes the study of:

  • Formation
  • Composition
  • Occurrence
  • Types
  • Associated properties
  • Uses of minerals

3. Petrology

Petro = Rock
Logy = Study

Therefore, Petrology is the study of rocks.

The Earth's crust is called the lithosphere and is made up of different types of rocks.

Petrology deals with:

  • Formation
  • Structure
  • Features
  • Composition
  • Occurrence
  • Types of rocks

4. Structural Geology

Structural Geology deals with rocks forming the Earth's crust that undergo deformation, distortion and disturbances under the influence of tectonic forces.

These result in geological structures such as:

  • Folds
  • Faults
  • Joints
  • Unconformities
Supporting detail added for clarity: Tectonic forces are forces associated with movement and deformation of the Earth's crust.
⭐ Faults, folds and joints are particularly important in engineering because they can affect the stability of rock masses.

5. Historical Geology

The Earth's surface provides suitable conditions for deposition of sediments at different places. Sedimentary rocks can therefore preserve evidence representing different periods of Earth's history.

Investigation of these rocks can reveal the chronological sequence of:

  • Formation of rocks
  • Evolution of life
  • Migration of plants and animals
  • Life during different periods of Earth's history
The study of Earth's history through geological records, particularly sedimentary rocks, is called Historical Geology.

6. Palaeontology

Under favourable conditions, animals and plants may become embedded in sediments and may be preserved partly or completely.

Naturally preserved remnants or evidence of ancient life occurring in rocks are known as fossils.

Palaeontology deals with:

  • Mode of formation of fossils
  • Types of fossils
  • Occurrence of fossils
Supporting detail added for clarity: A fossil is preserved evidence or remains of organisms that lived in the geological past.

7. Economic Geology

The prosperity of a nation depends to a considerable extent on economically useful mineral deposits.

Economic Geology deals with geological materials and mineral deposits having economic importance.

Example from the notes: Countries rich in oil deposits.

8. Engineering Geology

⭐ Very Important
Engineering Geology deals with the application of geological knowledge in the field of Civil Engineering for execution of safe, stable and economical constructions such as dams, bridges and tunnels.
  • Dams
  • Bridges
  • Tunnels

9. Mining Geology

Mining Geology deals with the application of geological knowledge in the field of mining.

A mining engineer should know:

  • Mode of occurrence of ores
  • Associations of ores
  • Properties of ores

Important physical parameters mentioned in the notes are:

  • Depth
  • Direction — Strike
  • Inclination — Dip
  • Thickness
Supporting detail added for clarity: Strike describes the direction of a geological plane, while dip describes its inclination from the horizontal.

10. Geophysics

Geophysics deals with the Earth's crust and physical properties of the Earth such as density and magnetism.

Classification

Pure Geophysics

Deals with general aspects of the Earth as a whole.

Exploration Geophysics

Deals with investigation of the upper layers of the Earth's crust.

11. Geo-hydrology / Hydrogeology

Geo-hydrology is also called Hydrogeology.

It deals with groundwater, including its:

  • Occurrence
  • Movement
  • Nature
  • Quality
  • Quantity

Groundwater behaviour is related to properties such as:

  • Porosity
  • Permeability
  • Structure
  • Texture
  • Composition of geological material

12. Geochemistry

Geochemistry deals with the chemical composition of minerals and rocks.

It also deals with:

  • Occurrence
  • Distribution
  • Abundance
  • Mobility

⭐ Importance of Geology in Civil Engineering

  1. Before construction, proper site selection is important from the point of view of foundation stability.
  2. Geology provides systematic knowledge of construction materials and their properties.
  3. Knowledge about the nature of rocks is useful in tunnelling and construction of roads.
  4. Foundation problems of dams, bridges and buildings are directly related to the geology of the area.
  5. Knowledge of groundwater is necessary for excavation works, water supply, irrigation and other purposes.
  6. Knowledge of erosion, transportation and deposition by surface water helps in soil conservation and river control.
  7. Geological maps and sections considerably help in planning engineering projects.
  8. Geological features such as faults, folds and joints must be suitably treated to maintain stability of rock structures.
  9. A pre-geological survey of the concerned area reduces the cost of planning work.

TOPIC 02

Geological Agents

Geological agents and the distinction between exogenous and endogenous processes.

Geological Agents

Geological agents are natural forces or processes responsible for visible/geological changes on or within the Earth.

Classification

1. Exogenous Geological Agents

Agents/processes acting mainly at or near the Earth's surface.

2. Endogenous Geological Agents

Agents/processes originating below the Earth's surface.

1. Exogenous Geological Agents

According to the notes, agents originating at the Earth's surface work slowly but steadily and erase topographical irregularities or relief.

Their geological work occurs systematically through:

Erosion → Transportation → Deposition

Erosion

Erosion involves removal or wearing away of earth/rock material.

Transportation

Transportation is the movement of eroded material from one place to another.

Deposition

Deposition is the settling or accumulation of transported material.

The notes refer to rivers and their large-scale transportation of sediments as an example of geological work.

⭐ Remember the sequence: Erosion → Transportation → Deposition

2. Endogenous Geological Agents

The nature, origin and function of endogenous geological agents are opposite to surface-originating exogenous agents.

They originate below the Earth's surface and can act suddenly, creating topographical irregularities.

Examples mentioned around this section of the notes include:

  • Volcanoes
  • Earthquakes
  • Tectonic forces
  • Tsunamis
Clarification: Groundwater is also written in the example line in the notes. In standard geological classification, groundwater is normally treated as an external geological agent. It is therefore preserved as a note reference here but is not classified as an endogenous process in this portal.
2 MARKS

Define geological agents and give their classification.

Answer: Geological agents are natural forces/processes responsible for geological changes on or within the Earth. They are classified into (1) Exogenous geological agents and (2) Endogenous geological agents.

Answer:
  1. Erosion: Removal or wearing away of earth/rock material.

Sequence: Erosion → Transportation → Deposition.

TOPIC 03

Weathering of Rocks

Definition, classification and the mechanical-weathering material covered in the Week 01 notes.

Weathering of Rocks

Weathering is the process by which rocks are broken down and decomposed by the action of natural agencies such as wind, rivers, rain and temperature changes.

Classification of Weathering

  1. Mechanical Weathering
  2. Chemical Weathering
  3. Biological Weathering
⭐ Remember the three-part classification: Mechanical • Chemical • Biological

1. Mechanical Weathering

Mechanical weathering involves the physical breakdown of rocks into smaller pieces due to natural forces.

The types listed in the notes are:

  1. Frost wedging
  2. Expansion and contraction
  3. Effects of vegetation
Expansion and contraction and effects of vegetation are listed in the uploaded notes, but their detailed explanations are not present in the six Week 01 pages. They are therefore not expanded here as teacher-provided material.

⭐ Frost Wedging

Water present in cracks of rocks freezes during cold conditions and melts when temperature rises.

Freezing of water causes an increase in volume. This produces pressure against the walls of the rock cracks.

Repeated freezing and thawing gradually widens the cracks and ultimately causes the rock to break down.

Water enters cracks → Freezing → Expansion → Pressure on crack walls → Crack widening → Rock breakdown
⭐ Exam sequence: Water in cracks → Freezing → Expansion → Pressure → Widening of cracks → Rock disintegration.

⭐ Expansion and Contraction

Rocks exposed at the Earth's surface undergo repeated heating during the day and cooling during the night.

On heating, the minerals present in rocks expand, while on cooling they contract. Different minerals may expand and contract at different rates.

Repeated expansion and contraction produces internal stresses in the rock. With time, cracks develop and the outer layers may loosen and separate, causing mechanical disintegration.

Heating → Expansion → Cooling → Contraction → Repeated stress → Cracking → Rock disintegration
⭐ Exam Point: Repeated temperature changes cause alternate expansion and contraction of rocks, producing stresses that gradually break the rock into smaller pieces.

⭐ Effect of Vegetation

Vegetation can contribute to the mechanical breakdown of rocks. Roots of plants and trees may enter small cracks and joints present in rocks.

As the roots grow, they become thicker and exert pressure on the walls of the cracks. This pressure gradually widens the cracks and may eventually split the rock.

Roots enter cracks → Root growth → Pressure on crack walls → Widening of cracks → Rock breakdown
⭐ Remember: Root growth acts like a wedge inside rock cracks and contributes to mechanical weathering.

2. Chemical Weathering

Chemical Weathering is the decomposition or alteration of rocks through chemical reactions involving water and dissolved substances.

In chemical weathering, the mineral composition of the rock is changed. The important processes covered in the notes are:

  1. Solution
  2. Leaching
  3. Hydration

Solution

Solution is a chemical-weathering process in which soluble minerals present in rocks dissolve in water.

Water moving through rocks removes the dissolved mineral matter and gradually weakens or decomposes the rock.

Water contacts rock → Soluble minerals dissolve → Dissolved material is removed → Rock weakens

Leaching

Leaching is the removal of soluble constituents from rocks or soil by water percolating through them.

Rainwater or groundwater dissolves soluble materials and carries them downward or away from their original position.

Water percolation → Dissolution of soluble constituents → Transportation by water → Removal from rock or soil
⭐ Key Point: Leaching involves both dissolution and removal of soluble constituents by percolating water.

Hydration

Hydration is a chemical-weathering process in which water molecules combine with minerals present in rocks.

Addition of water may increase the volume of the mineral and change its physical and chemical properties. The resulting expansion and weakening assist in the decomposition of rock.

Mineral + Water → Hydrated mineral → Volume/property change → Weakening of rock

3. Biological Weathering

Biological Weathering is the breakdown or decomposition of rocks due to the activities of living organisms.

Biological agents may include:

  • Plant roots
  • Burrowing organisms
  • Microorganisms
  • Human activities

Plant roots can widen existing cracks mechanically, while organisms and decaying organic matter can also promote chemical alteration of rock material.

⭐ Important distinction: Vegetation can contribute to both mechanical and biological weathering. Root pressure physically widens cracks, while biological activity can also promote chemical decomposition.

🌍 Structure of the Earth

Structure of the Earth: The Earth consists of several distinct layers and regions having different physical and chemical characteristics.

For understanding geological processes, the important parts considered here are the atmosphere, lithosphere, crust, mantle and core.

Earth → Atmosphere → Lithosphere → Crust → Mantle → Core
⭐ Engineering Geology Importance: Knowledge of the Earth's structure is essential for understanding rocks, earthquakes, volcanoes and plate-tectonic processes.

🌤️ Atmosphere and its Layers

Atmosphere: The atmosphere is the envelope of gases surrounding the Earth.

The atmosphere is divided into different layers mainly according to the variation of temperature with altitude.

The atmospheric layers covered in this topic are:

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Thermosphere
Earth's Surface → Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere

1. Troposphere

Troposphere: The troposphere is the lowest layer of the Earth's atmosphere.

It extends upward from the Earth's surface and contains most of the atmospheric water vapour.

Most weather phenomena such as clouds, rainfall, winds and storms occur in the troposphere.

In general, temperature decreases with increase in altitude in this layer.

⭐ Remember: Troposphere = Lowest atmospheric layer + major weather activity.

2. Stratosphere

Stratosphere: The stratosphere is the atmospheric layer situated above the troposphere.

The ozone-rich region occurs mainly within the stratosphere. Ozone absorbs a large part of the harmful ultraviolet radiation received from the Sun.

Unlike the troposphere, temperature generally increases with altitude in the stratosphere because of the absorption of ultraviolet radiation by ozone.

⭐ Important: The ozone layer is mainly associated with the stratosphere.

3. Mesosphere

Mesosphere: The mesosphere is the atmospheric layer situated above the stratosphere.

Temperature generally decreases with increasing altitude in this layer.

Most meteors entering the Earth's atmosphere burn up in the mesosphere due to interaction with atmospheric particles.

⭐ Remember: Many meteors burn up in the mesosphere.

4. Thermosphere

Thermosphere: The thermosphere is the atmospheric layer situated above the mesosphere.

Temperature increases considerably with altitude because atmospheric particles absorb high-energy solar radiation.

A significant part of the ionosphere lies within the thermosphere. Ionised particles in this region are important in atmospheric and radio-wave phenomena.

⭐ Remember: Thermosphere = Very high temperatures in the upper atmosphere.

🌎 Lithosphere

Lithosphere: The lithosphere is the rigid outermost mechanical layer of the Earth. It consists of the crust and the rigid uppermost part of the mantle.

The lithosphere is not one continuous unbroken shell. It is divided into a number of large and small rigid pieces called tectonic plates.

These plates move slowly relative to one another over the weaker material beneath the lithosphere.

⭐ Important Correction:
Lithosphere ≠ Crust only.
Lithosphere = Crust + Rigid Uppermost Mantle.

Crust

Crust: The crust is the outermost compositional layer of the solid Earth.

It is very thin compared with the total radius of the Earth and forms the continents and ocean basins.

The Earth's crust is broadly divided into:

  1. Oceanic Crust
  2. Continental Crust
Earth's Crust → Oceanic Crust + Continental Crust

🌊 Oceanic Crust and Continental Crust

The two principal types of crust differ in their thickness, density and general composition.

Property Oceanic Crust Continental Crust
Occurrence Forms the floor of ocean basins Forms the continents
Thickness Generally thinner Generally thicker
Density Generally denser Generally less dense
General composition Predominantly basaltic More granitic in overall character
⭐ Exam Point:
Oceanic crust → thinner and denser.
Continental crust → thicker and generally less dense.

Mantle

Mantle: The mantle is the thick layer of the Earth situated below the crust and above the core.

It forms the largest part of the Earth's volume and consists predominantly of silicate rocks rich in magnesium and iron.

The mantle is mainly solid, but over geological time some mantle material can deform and flow very slowly.

This slow movement of mantle material is closely related to plate-tectonic processes.

⭐ Do not write: "The mantle is completely liquid."
Most of the mantle is solid but behaves plastically over very long geological periods.

🔥 Core

Core: The core is the innermost major compositional region of the Earth. It consists predominantly of iron and nickel.

The Earth's core is divided into two parts:

  1. Outer Core
  2. Inner Core
Core → Outer Core + Inner Core

Outer Core

Outer Core: The outer core is the liquid portion of the Earth's core surrounding the solid inner core.

It consists mainly of iron and nickel and exists in a liquid state.

Motion of electrically conducting material in the liquid outer core plays the principal role in generating the Earth's magnetic field.

⭐ Outer Core = Liquid.

Inner Core

Inner Core: The inner core is the solid central part of the Earth.

It consists predominantly of iron and nickel.

Although the temperature at the centre of the Earth is extremely high, the inner core remains solid because of the enormous pressure acting at that depth.

⭐ Inner Core = Solid.
Earth's Interior → Crust → Mantle → Outer Core (Liquid) → Inner Core (Solid)

📌 Quick Revision — Structure of the Earth

Atmosphere: Gaseous envelope surrounding Earth.

Troposphere: Lowest layer; most weather occurs here.

Stratosphere: Contains the ozone-rich region.

Mesosphere: Many meteors burn up here.

Thermosphere: Temperature rises greatly with altitude.

Lithosphere: Crust + rigid uppermost mantle.

Crust: Outermost compositional layer of the solid Earth.

Mantle: Thick layer between crust and core; mainly solid.

Outer Core: Liquid.

Inner Core: Solid.

🌍 Plate Tectonics Theory

Plate Tectonics Theory: Plate tectonics is the theory that the Earth's lithosphere is divided into a number of rigid plates which move slowly relative to one another over the weaker material beneath them.

The theory of plate tectonics explains many major geological phenomena such as earthquakes, volcanic activity, mountain formation, oceanic trenches and the formation of new crust.

The movement and interaction of tectonic plates are especially important at their boundaries.

⭐ Key Point: The lithosphere is divided into moving tectonic plates.

Tectonic Plates

Tectonic Plates: Tectonic plates are large, rigid pieces of the Earth's lithosphere that move slowly relative to one another.

A tectonic plate may contain continental lithosphere, oceanic lithosphere, or a combination of both.

The relative movement of these plates produces different types of plate boundaries.

Lithosphere → Tectonic Plates → Plate Movement → Interaction at Plate Boundaries

Plate Boundaries

Plate Boundary: A plate boundary is the region where two tectonic plates meet and interact with each other.

Plate boundaries are classified according to the direction of relative movement of the plates.

There are three principal types of plate boundaries:

  1. Convergent Boundary
  2. Divergent Boundary
  3. Transform Boundary
Plate Boundaries → Convergent + Divergent + Transform

📌 Types of Plate Boundaries — Simple Diagram

1. Convergent Boundary

Plate A  ───────→ ←───────  Plate B
                 ↑
          Plates move together

2. Divergent Boundary

Plate A  ←─────── ───────→  Plate B
                 ↑

        ───────────── Boundary
              ←───────  Plate B

      Plates slide past each other
⭐ Easy Memory:
Convergent → Come together
Divergent → Move apart
Transform → Slide past

1. Convergent Boundary

Convergent Boundary: A convergent plate boundary is a boundary at which two tectonic plates move towards each other.
Plate → ← Plate

When plates converge, compression occurs. Depending on the types of plates involved, one plate may descend beneath another or the collision may produce crustal deformation and mountain building.

Convergent boundaries are commonly associated with:

  • Subduction
  • Oceanic trenches
  • Volcanic activity
  • Earthquakes
  • Mountain building
⭐ Remember: Convergent Boundary = Plates move towards each other.

Types of Convergent Plate Interaction

Depending on the nature of the plates involved, convergence may occur between:

  1. Oceanic Plate and Continental Plate
  2. Oceanic Plate and Oceanic Plate
  3. Continental Plate and Continental Plate
⭐ When a denser oceanic plate converges with a less-dense plate, the oceanic plate commonly sinks beneath the other plate, producing a subduction zone.

🌋 Subduction Zone

Subduction Zone: A subduction zone is a region at a convergent plate boundary where one tectonic plate sinks beneath another plate and descends into the mantle.

Subduction commonly occurs when a dense oceanic plate converges with another plate. 

            Continental Plate
    ─────────────────────────────
                  ←
                   \
                    \
Oceanic Plate →      \  Subducting Plate
──────────────────────\────────────
                       \
                        \ ↓
                         Mantle
Plates converge → Denser plate bends downward → Plate descends beneath another plate → Subduction Zone

Subduction zones are important regions of earthquakes, volcanic activity and deep-ocean trench formation.

⭐ Important: Subduction is associated with a convergent plate boundary.

2. Divergent Boundary

Divergent Boundary: A divergent plate boundary is a boundary at which two tectonic plates move away from each other.
Plate ← → Plate

As the plates separate, material from below may rise into the opening. Cooling and solidification of this material can produce new crust.

Divergent boundaries are commonly associated with:

  • Formation of new crust
  • Mid-ocean ridges
  • Rift zones
  • Volcanic activity
  • Shallow earthquakes
          ← Plate       Plate →
    ─────────────       ─────────────
              \         /
               \   ↑   /
                \  │  /
                 Magma
⭐ Remember: Divergent Boundary = Plates move away from each other.

3. Transform Boundary

Transform Boundary: A transform plate boundary is a boundary at which two tectonic plates move horizontally past each other.

At a transform boundary, crust is generally neither created nor destroyed.

Friction may prevent smooth movement of the plates. Stress can therefore accumulate and may be released suddenly in the form of earthquakes. 

        Plate A  ─────────→
    ───────────────────────── Boundary
              ←─────────  Plate B
⭐ Remember: Transform Boundary = Plates slide horizontally past each other.

📊 Comparison of Plate Boundaries

Boundary Plate Movement Main Effect
Convergent Towards each other Collision, subduction, mountain building, earthquakes and volcanism
Divergent Away from each other New crust, ridges, rifts, volcanism and shallow earthquakes
Transform Slide past each other Faulting and earthquakes; crust is generally neither created nor destroyed

Plate Tectonics: Theory describing the movement and interaction of rigid lithospheric plates.

Tectonic Plate: A large rigid piece of the lithosphere.

Plate Boundary: Region where tectonic plates meet and interact.

Convergent Boundary: Plates move towards each other.

Divergent Boundary: Plates move away from each other.

Subduction Zone: Region where one plate descends beneath another at a convergent boundary.

  1. Convergent Boundary: Two plates move towards each other. Subduction or continental collision may occur.
  2. Divergent Boundary: Two plates move away from each other. New crust can form as magma rises and solidifies.
  3. Transform Boundary: Two plates slide horizontally past each other. Crust is generally neither created nor destroyed.
2 MARKS

What is a subduction zone?

Answer: A subduction zone is a region at a convergent plate boundary where one tectonic plate descends beneath another plate into the mantle.
3 MARKS

Differentiate between convergent, divergent and transform boundaries.

Answer:
Boundary Plate Movement Main Effect
Convergent Towards each other Collision or subduction
Divergent Away from each other Formation of new crust
Transform Slide past each other Faulting and earthquakes
